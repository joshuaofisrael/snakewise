Where do snakes live?

Snakes live on every continent except Antarctica, in rainforests, deserts, grasslands, wetlands, mountains and tropical oceans. Rainforests hold the most species, while a few, like the European adder, survive north of the Arctic Circle by brumating through winter.

Where do snakes live? Everywhere but the coldest places

Snakes live on every continent except Antarctica. Ireland, Iceland, New Zealand and some other islands have no native land snakes, largely due to isolation and past glaciation.

Tropical rainforests: the richest snake habitats

The richest snake habitats on Earth. Tree boas, vine snakes, pit vipers and huge pythons and anacondas thrive in warm, humid forests of the Amazon, Congo Basin and Southeast Asia.

Deserts: how snakes cope with heat

Sidewinders, horned vipers and sand boas cope with heat by being active at night and burrowing into cool sand.

Grasslands and savannas: snakes of the prairies and savannas

Abundant rodents support rattlesnakes in North American prairies and mambas, puff adders and pythons in African savannas.

Wetlands and rivers: snakes that hunt in water

Water snakes, cottonmouths and anacondas hunt fish and amphibians along rivers, marshes and swamps.

Not every wetland snake belongs there: invasive Burmese pythons have become a major predator in South Florida, including the Everglades. Why removing large female pythons matters most.

Oceans: where sea snakes live

Around 70 species of sea snakes live in warm Indian and Pacific waters. Most give birth to live young at sea.

Cold climates: how far north snakes live

The common European adder (Vipera berus) ranges north of the Arctic Circle in Scandinavia, surviving long winters by hibernating (brumation).
