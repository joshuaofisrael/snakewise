Snake species facts: the longest, heaviest and most venomous snakes

There are around 4,000 snake species. The reticulated python is often cited as the longest, the green anaconda is the heaviest, the king cobra is the longest venomous snake, and the inland taipan has the most toxic venom of any land snake in lab tests. The table and profiles below compare 12 notable species.

King cobra

Ophiophagus hannah · South & Southeast Asia

The world's longest venomous snake, reaching over 5 m. It eats mainly other snakes and is the only snake known to build a nest for its eggs. Read how king cobras build nests.

Reticulated python

Malayopython reticulatus · Southeast Asia

Often cited as the longest snake species, with records over 6–7 m. A non-venomous constrictor and strong swimmer.

Green anaconda

Eunectes murinus · South America

The heaviest snake, potentially exceeding 200 kg. Spends much time in water, ambushing prey at the edge.

Black mamba

Dendroaspis polylepis · Sub-Saharan Africa

Named for the black inside of its mouth, not its grey-brown body. One of the fastest snakes, but shy and prefers to flee.

Inland taipan

Oxyuranus microlepidotus · Central Australia

Has the most toxic venom of any land snake in lab tests, yet is reclusive and bites on people are very rare.

Gaboon viper

Bitis gabonica · Central & West Africa

Has the longest fangs of any snake (up to about 5 cm) and superb leaf-litter camouflage.

Eastern diamondback rattlesnake

Crotalus adamanteus · Southeastern USA

The heaviest venomous snake in the Americas. Its rattle is made of interlocking keratin segments added at each shed.

Corn snake

Pantherophis guttatus · Southeastern USA

A gentle, non-venomous rodent-eater and one of the most popular pet snakes.

Brahminy blind snake

Indotyphlops braminus · Worldwide (spread by people)

A tiny burrower resembling an earthworm. All known individuals are female and reproduce without males (parthenogenesis). Read how this snake reproduces without mating.

Yellow-bellied sea snake

Hydrophis platurus · Tropical Indian & Pacific Oceans

Lives its whole life at sea, drifting along ocean slicks. Can absorb some oxygen through its skin. Read how sea snakes breathe through their skin.

Paradise tree snake

Chrysopelea paradisi · Southeast Asia

A 'flying' snake that glides between trees by flattening its body and undulating in the air. Read the full paradise flying snake profile.

Barbados threadsnake

Tetracheilostoma carlae · Barbados

One of the smallest known snakes, roughly 10 cm long, about the size of a noodle. Read about the smallest snake in the world.

Snake species FAQ

How many snake species are there?

There are around 4,000 snake species.

What is the longest snake in the world?

The reticulated python is often cited as the longest snake species, with records over 6–7 m. It is a non-venomous constrictor and strong swimmer from Southeast Asia.

What is the heaviest snake?

The green anaconda of South America is the heaviest snake, potentially exceeding 200 kg.

What is the longest venomous snake?

The king cobra is the world's longest venomous snake, reaching over 5 m. It eats mainly other snakes and is the only snake known to build a nest for its eggs.

Which land snake has the most toxic venom?

The inland taipan of Central Australia has the most toxic venom of any land snake in lab tests, yet it is reclusive and bites on people are very rare.

What is the smallest snake?

The Barbados threadsnake is one of the smallest known snakes, roughly 10 cm long, about the size of a noodle.

Which snake has the longest fangs?

The Gaboon viper of Central and West Africa has the longest fangs of any snake, up to about 5 cm.
