Editorial policy

SnakeWise publishes plain-language pages about snake biology, species, identification and safety. This page is how those pages are written, sourced and corrected.

By the SnakeWise editorial team · Last updated: 20 November 2026

Who writes SnakeWise

Articles are bylined to the SnakeWise editorial team, which is the site itself, not a named outside expert. We do not invent personal biographies, academic titles or field credentials for individual writers. Ownership details sit only on the Terms, Privacy Policy and Disclaimer. For a short description of the site, see About.

What a page has to do

Each article answers one question. The title should match the question a reader actually typed, and the first paragraph should answer it. A species note is published only when it has facts that belong to that species: range, size, venom status or lookalikes, each tied to a source. We do not ship a page that is the same paragraph with the species name swapped.

Guides link onward. A blog post should point to the species page, the venomous-or-not guide and the safety page when those pages are the deeper version of something the article mentions. Related reading sits in the article, with the other page's own picture, not in a block that looks like an advertisement.

Sources

A factual claim needs a source we opened. For a study, that is the paper, reached through its DOI or a PubMed record, not a retelling on another site. The citation on the page names the authors, the year, the journal and the DOI.

We also use, as support rather than as the only source for a measurement, the Reptile Database, Smithsonian National Zoo species accounts, university herpetology pages, state wildlife agency pages and World Health Organization snakebite pages. We do not use Wikipedia, a pet-shop blog, a Guinness entry or an AI summary as the sole source for a number. If a paper's abstract will not open, the claim that depended on it is left out.

We do not invent measurements, quotations or "a scientist said" lines. A myth with no study behind it is labelled as a myth, as on the myths page.

Snakebite and identification

Pages about bites and identification put the emergency step first: call local emergency services or poison control. SnakeWise does not give drug doses, and it does not tell a reader to cut a bite, suck venom, apply ice or use a tourniquet.

We never say that a snake in a photograph, or a snake in someone's yard, is safe to pick up. Colour, head shape and rhymes are unreliable. If a page is unsure, it says to treat the snake as venomous and keep distance. Venom labels on the site may be wrong: a figure from one mouse test is not a ranking of danger to people, and lookalike species are common. The safety page and the ID guide are the places those limits are spelled out.

Dates

The published date on an article is the day it first went up. It is not the day the site was rebuilt. When we re-open the sources and change the page, we change the "last reviewed" line and the dateModified field, and we say which day the sources were checked. A page is not marked newer just to look fresh.

Corrections

If a source has moved, a number is wrong, or a species account has been revised, use the contact form. Say which page and which sentence. We correct the page and update the reviewed date. We would rather delete a sentence than leave a claim we can no longer check.

Images

Photo-style pictures are either AI-generated illustrations or real photographs used under a licence named on the credits page. AI-generated pictures are captioned as AI-generated and the caption links to the image policy. They are not photographs of a real snake and must not be used to identify one. We do not present a generated picture as a news photograph of a real event.

Advertising

SnakeWise may show third-party ads, including Google AdSense. Articles are not written to push those ads. We do not ask anyone to click an ad, and we do not style a site link to look like an ad button. Ads are not part of the article, the sources or the safety advice.

What this page is not

This is an editorial standard, not medical, veterinary or legal advice. The disclaimer covers the limits of the site. Questions about a picture, a licence or a missing credit go to the contact form as well.
