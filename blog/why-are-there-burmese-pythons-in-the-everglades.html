Why Are There Burmese Pythons in the Everglades?

Burmese pythons are in the Everglades because pet snakes escaped or were released there. The U.S. Geological Survey traces the wild population to a small number of pet pythons in the southern Everglades in the early 1980s, and the species has since spread across southern Florida.

They came from the pet trade

The U.S. Geological Survey says Burmese pythons were imported to the United States for the pet trade for many decades. It says the initial wild population is believed to have started from a small number of escaped or released pet pythons at the southern end of Everglades National Park in the early 1980s. The National Park Service gives the same explanation: the snakes are established in the park because captive pets were accidentally or intentionally released.

What about Hurricane Andrew?

Hurricane Andrew often gets the blame, but it was not the original introduction. The Florida Museum says the first sighting of a python in the Everglades was between 1980 and 1990, and that Hurricane Andrew in 1992, which destroyed a breeding facility, likely made the spread worse. CBS News reports that the Category 5 storm struck south of Miami on August 23, 1992, damaged exotic animal facilities including a python breeding facility, and that a state inspection report said witnesses had spotted hundreds of large snakes loose. The Florida Museum says scientists confirmed a breeding population by the early 2000s.

Why the Everglades suited them

The U.S. Geological Survey says Burmese pythons prefer subtropical and tropical climates in their native range, and southern Florida offers a similar environment. The wetlands, shallow water, brush and even gopher tortoise burrows give them places to hide, and much of the Everglades is far from people, which makes the snakes hard to detect and remove.

The National Park Service adds that their coloring and behavior let them blend into the environment, so even large pythons are hard to find. That makes it very hard to count them.

What the pythons have done

The U.S. Geological Survey says the Florida population is expected to be on the scale of tens of thousands of snakes and appears to be growing. It reports that pythons are responsible for large drops in mammal numbers in the Everglades, with raccoons, opossums, bobcats, rabbits, gray foxes and white tailed deer declining by 85 to 100 percent. It says Burmese pythons in the wild often grow to about 16 feet, and the largest recorded in Florida was a female of about 19 feet.

Why removal is so hard

The U.S. Geological Survey says people searching on foot or by road find only a small share of pythons, with detection rates under 5 percent, and that traps designed for pythons have not been reliable. It also says the odds of eradicating an introduced reptile population once it has spread across a large area are very low. The National Park Service says it and partners have spent more than 10 years investigating ways to remove pythons, including an authorized agent removal program. You can read about one removal data study in our article on the Burmese python removal index.

What this means for snake owners

The Everglades story is a warning about releasing pets. A pet python that outgrows its home should be rehomed through a reptile rescue or a vet, not released into the wild. If you see a large python in the wild, keep your distance and report it to local wildlife authorities. See our pet snake basics before buying any large snake.

FAQ

How did Burmese pythons get to the Everglades?

Through the pet trade. Escaped or released pet pythons started the wild population in the early 1980s, according to the U.S. Geological Survey.

Did Hurricane Andrew cause the python problem?

Not on its own. Pythons were already in the Everglades by the 1980s, but the Florida Museum says the hurricane, which destroyed a breeding facility, likely worsened the spread.

How many Burmese pythons are in Florida?

The U.S. Geological Survey says the population is expected to be tens of thousands. An exact number is not known.

Can the pythons be removed from the Everglades?

The U.S. Geological Survey says the odds of eradicating them are very low, because the snakes are hard to detect and traps have not been reliable.

How big do Burmese pythons get in Florida?

Often about 16 feet, with the largest recorded in Florida a female of about 19 feet, according to the U.S. Geological Survey.
