Do Snakes Have Necks? What a Snake's Spine Really Looks Like

Yes. A snake's spine has a neck region just behind the head, but the change to the trunk is gradual, so you cannot see where it ends.

Do snakes have necks?

Yes, in the sense that matters to anatomists. A snake's backbone is not one long, uniform tube. When Jason Head and P. David Polly measured the vertebrae along the spines of snakes and limbed lizards, they found that snake spines are divided into distinct regions, much as a lizard's is. The region just behind the head behaves like a neck, even though there is no shoulder or front leg to mark where it ends (Head & Polly, 2015).

What snakes lack is an obvious outward boundary. In a lizard, the front legs show you where the neck stops and the chest begins. In a snake, the change is gradual and hidden inside the body, so the neck is real but hard to see.

How many bones are in a snake's spine?

Far more than in our own. People have 33 vertebrae, while snakes commonly have a few hundred, and almost every vertebra in the trunk carries a pair of ribs. That long chain of small, similar joints is what lets a snake bend into tight curves, climb, swim and coil. You can read more about how those curves are used in our guide to how snakes move.

How does a snake end up with so many vertebrae?

Vertebrae form in the embryo from repeating blocks of tissue called somites. Each block is laid down in time with a molecular "segmentation clock". Work on corn snake embryos showed that this clock ticks much faster, relative to overall growth, than in mice, chicks or fish, so the embryo produces many more, smaller segments before growth of the body axis stops (Gomez et al., 2008). More ticks means more vertebrae.

Why did scientists think snakes had no neck?

An older idea held that snakes lost their regions altogether, so that the whole trunk became one long "chest" region. Gene expression studies had suggested that the genes which normally mark out spine regions were behaving unusually in snakes. Head and Polly's measurements of the bones themselves told a different story: the regions are still there, just with very gradual changes between them. Their conclusion was that the snake body plan evolved by stretching and blurring existing regions rather than erasing them (Head & Polly, 2015).

Does a long spine make snakes fragile?

Not usually. Each snake vertebra has extra interlocking joint surfaces that limit twisting, which helps the column resist damage while still bending side to side. That said, rough handling can injure a snake's spine, which is one reason the advice on our snake safety page is to leave wild snakes alone and let trained people move them.

FAQ

Where does a snake's neck end?

There is no sharp line. The change from neck vertebrae to trunk vertebrae is gradual, which is why the neck is hard to spot from outside (Head & Polly, 2015).

Do snakes have ribs all the way down?

Ribs run along almost the whole trunk. The tail, behind the vent, has vertebrae but no ribs.

Do snakes have more vertebrae than humans?

Yes, many more. Humans have 33, and snakes commonly have a few hundred.
