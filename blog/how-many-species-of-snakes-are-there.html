How Many Species of Snakes Are There? About 4,250 and Counting

The Reptile Database lists 4,252 snake species when searched on 11 November 2026. That is a snapshot, because the number rises as new species are described and shifts when scientists revise how snakes are classified.

The current count

The Reptile Database is a taxonomic database covering all living reptile species, including turtles, snakes, lizards and crocodiles. A search of its higher taxon "snake" on 11 November 2026 returned 4,252 species.

For context, the same database search returned 8,042 lizard species and 365 turtle species on that date. Snakes are therefore a much bigger group than turtles but about half the size of lizards in this database.

Why you will see different numbers

Published figures vary because the count is a moving target. Scientists keep describing new species, and taxonomists sometimes split one species into several or merge species together. A figure quoted in an older book or article may be out of date. The database itself notes record-breaking totals in successive releases: its December 2022 release reported 11,940 reptile species, up 120 from the release before.

When you cite a number, name the source and the date you checked it.

Where snakes fit among reptiles

By these counts snakes are the second largest of the three groups we checked, with a bit over half as many species as lizards and far more than turtles. For more on snake biology, see our snake species guide and our article on whether snakes have eyelids.

What the number does not tell you

The total says nothing about how many snakes are dangerous. Only a minority of snake species are a medical concern, and we cover safety on our safety page. Never approach or handle a wild snake.
