How Long Can a Snake Go Without Eating? What Pythons Reveal

Many snakes can go months without eating, and some are reported to survive up to about two years. They manage it by cutting their resting metabolism sharply and burning stored fat while sparing protein. When a Burmese python finally eats, its gut and heart grow within days, then shrink back once the meal is digested.

Published 11 October 2026 · Last reviewed 11 October 2026

Quick facts

  • Short answer: many snakes can go months without food, and some are reported to survive up to about two years. In one lab study, ball pythons, ratsnakes and western diamondback rattlesnakes all came through 168 days (about five and a half months) of complete fasting.
  • How they do it: they slow their resting metabolism (by up to 72% in that study) and burn fat while protecting their protein.
  • Star species: the Burmese python (Python bivittatus), native to South and Southeast Asia from Nepal and India to southern China and Indonesia, and introduced to Florida.
  • The twist: when a python finally eats, its gut and heart grow fast, then shrink back once the meal is digested.

How long can a snake go without eating?

Longer than almost any other land vertebrate its size. Physiologist M. D. McCue noted in 2007 that some snakes are known to survive periods without food lasting up to two years. In his own experiment, he kept ball pythons, ratsnakes and western diamondback rattlesnakes without food for 168 days. All three species tolerated it, even though they come from distantly related branches of the snake family tree.

That matters for how we picture snakes. A snake that skips meals for weeks is not necessarily in trouble. Many wild snakes are ambush hunters that sit and wait, and a single big catch may have to last a long time. Their bodies are built for feast and famine rather than three meals a day.

How do snakes survive so long without food?

McCue described two strategies, borrowing words from economics.

  • Cutting demand. Fasting snakes lowered their resting metabolic rate by up to 72%. Spending less energy on simply staying alive means their stored fuel lasts far longer.
  • Managing supply. The snakes burned fat first and spared their protein, which is the material their muscles and organs are built from. Burning muscle for fuel would weaken them, so holding on to it keeps them able to move, hunt and recover.

McCue suggested that this talent for running on very little might help explain why snakes have persisted for so long and spread into low-energy environments where prey is scarce.

What happens inside a python when it finally eats?

The Burmese python is the classic example of a feast after a famine. In a 1995 study, Stephen Secor and Jared Diamond fed pythons meals weighing a quarter of the snake's own body mass and followed what happened over the next 30 days.

  • Within one to three days, the snakes' oxygen use peaked at 17 times its fasting level. This rise in metabolism after a meal, called specific dynamic action, was the largest reported for any vertebrate.
  • The rate at which the small intestine took up nutrients rose to between 6 and 26 times fasting levels, and the mass of the small intestine doubled. Other organs also grew.
  • Digesting the meal cost the equivalent of 32% of the energy the prey contained.
  • Everything returned to fasting levels by the time the snake passed its droppings, 8 to 14 days after eating.

In other words, a fasting python shuts much of its digestive system down to save energy, then rebuilds it at high speed when food arrives. Secor and Diamond argued in a 1998 paper in Nature that these huge, fast swings make young pythons an ideal model for studying how vertebrate bodies regulate themselves.

Does a python's heart really grow after a meal?

Yes. In 2005, J. B. Andersen and colleagues at the University of California, Irvine reported that the ventricle muscle of Burmese pythons grew by about 40% just 48 hours after feeding, driven by increased activity of genes for muscle proteins. The growth was fully reversible. (The paper used the older name Python molurus; the Burmese python is now usually treated as its own species, Python bivittatus.)

A 2011 study in Science, led by C. A. Riquelme in Leslie Leinwand's lab, looked at how this works. The python heart grew because its muscle cells got bigger, not because new cells formed. Although the snakes' blood was loaded with fats after a meal, the heart did not store them. Instead, it switched on pathways for transporting and burning fatty acids and raised levels of a protective enzyme, superoxide dismutase. The team also found a combination of fatty acids in fed python blood plasma that triggered this healthy kind of heart growth when injected into pythons or into mice.

The heart then shrinks back as the snake goes back to fasting. A 2024 study in PNAS by Thomas Martin and colleagues found that a signalling protein called FoxO1 is held back while the heart grows and becomes active as it shrinks, alongside the cell's recycling process, autophagy. Working with heart cells treated with fed python plasma, they showed that switching FoxO1 on could prevent and reverse the growth. Researchers hope this kind of work could one day inform treatments for human heart disease, though that remains a goal rather than a result.

Should a pet snake go months without eating?

Not on purpose. The studies above show what snake bodies can withstand, not how keepers should feed them, and lab fasts were run under careful conditions. Some pet snakes do refuse food for a while, but a snake that stops eating, loses weight or seems unwell should be checked by a reptile vet. Our pet snake basics page covers the essentials of care.

FAQ

How long can a snake live without food?

Many snakes can go months without eating. Some are reported to survive up to about two years, and in one study three different species all survived 168 days of complete fasting.

Why can snakes go so long without eating?

They lower their resting metabolism, by up to 72% in one study, and they burn stored fat while sparing the protein in their muscles and organs.

How much do pythons eat at once?

Pythons can swallow very large meals. In one well-known study, Burmese pythons were fed meals equal to 25% of their own body weight, and digestion was finished within about 8 to 14 days.

Does a snake's heart grow when it eats?

In Burmese pythons, yes. Heart ventricle muscle grew by about 40% within 48 hours of a meal and later shrank back to its fasting size.

Is it normal for a pet snake to stop eating?

Some pet snakes refuse food for a time, but weight loss or other signs of illness are a reason to see a reptile vet rather than wait.
