How Do Egg-Eating Snakes Eat Eggs? Inside the Shell-Cracking Snake

African egg-eating snakes (Dasypeltis) swallow a bird egg whole, crack it inside their throat on bony spikes that point down from their backbone, swallow the yolk and white, then spit out the crushed shell. They can swallow eggs far bigger than other snakes their size, and they eat nothing else.

Published 19 October 2026 · Last reviewed 19 October 2026

Quick facts

  • Who: the African egg-eating snakes, genus Dasypeltis. The Reptile Database lists 19 species across most of Africa and into south-western Arabia.
  • Diet: bird eggs and nothing else. They are among the only snakes in the world that live on eggs alone (Gartner & Greene, 2008).
  • The trick: the snake swallows the egg whole, cracks it inside its throat on bony spikes from its backbone, swallows the contents and spits out the shell.
  • Danger to people: none. They have only a few small teeth and no venom, yet they put on a convincing viper act.

How do egg-eating snakes eat eggs?

In three steps: swallow, crack, spit. The snake swallows the egg whole. A short way down the neck, bony points on the underside of the backbone stick down into loose folds of the throat lining. Gartner and Greene (2008) describe these spikes, called hypapophyses, on the 17th through 38th vertebrae. They stop the egg sliding further into the gut and help crack the shell and the membrane inside it. The snake then swallows the yolk and white and brings back up the crushed shell.

The anatomy was first worked out in Carl Gans's classic 1952 study, as Gartner and Greene summarise. The head is modified too. Changes to the quadrate, supratemporal and dentary bones, plus a reduced number of teeth, are all thought to help it swallow eggs that are huge relative to its head. Crushing the shell also means the egg takes up far less room in the gut (Gartner & Greene, 2008).

How big an egg can an egg-eating snake swallow?

Astonishingly big. Gartner and Greene (2008) fed eggs of rising size (finch, quail, swallow and chicken) to captive egg-eaters, mostly Dasypeltis atra from Uganda, until each snake could not manage the next one. They ran the same test on the common kingsnake (Lampropeltis getula), an American snake that often eats eggs but has no special egg anatomy. Using their results, a snake with a 30 mm head could swallow an egg about 56 mm across if it were an egg-eater, but only about 16 mm across if it were a kingsnake. Only large kingsnakes could eat even small eggs, which tend to roll and slip out of an ordinary snake's mouth.

Applied to real birds, that gap is huge. The authors estimated that an adult egg-eater could eat the eggs of 98% of the ground and shrub-nesting bird species it meets in southern Africa (232 of 236), while a kingsnake could eat only 36% of those it meets in the eastern United States. A newer three-year study of the common egg-eater (Dasypeltis scabra) reached a similar figure: adults can swallow the eggs of 708 of 728 bird species (98%), and newborn snakes those of about half (Barends & Maritz, 2025).

Oddly, a big head is not the secret. Barends and Maritz (2021) found that the rhombic egg-eater has a smaller head for its body size than three nest-raiding generalists (boomslang, Cape cobra and mole snake). Specialised anatomy, not head size, lets it compete with them for eggs.

Do egg-eating snakes pick which eggs to eat?

Yes. In lab trials with five Dasypeltis species, Krupa and Slabach (2023) offered eggs ranging from tiny king quail eggs to turkey eggs. Large snakes ate every size but preferred the largest, hardest eggs, while the smallest snakes took only the smallest. Given a choice, the snakes favoured dove and pigeon eggs over quail eggs.

What do egg-eating snakes do when there are no eggs?

They wait it out. Birds lay mostly in spring and summer, so eggs become scarce in winter, especially small ones. In Barends and Maritz's (2025) colony, the snakes fed eagerly in the austral spring and summer, when wild birds lay most, and needed food equal to only 61% of their starting body mass per year to keep in condition. The authors link this to the low metabolic rates measured in Dasypeltis. Compare how long snakes can go without eating.

Why did a snake evolve to eat only eggs?

Because Africa has plenty of small, reachable eggs. Gartner and Greene (2008) found that ground-nesting birds in southern Africa lay, on average, smaller eggs than those in the eastern United States, and that the big radiation of small African birds such as finches and weavers is roughly as old as the snakes' own lineage. Across snakes as a whole, de Queiroz and Rodríguez-Robles (2006) found that egg eating tends to arise in lineages that already eat the parent animals. Bird-egg eating appears in bird-eating snake lineages, perhaps because eggs smell like the adults.

Are egg-eating snakes venomous?

No, but they bluff brilliantly. When threatened, an egg-eater folds its body into parallel coils, makes mock strikes and rubs its keeled scales together to make a rasping hiss. That display copies saw-scaled vipers (Echis) and horned vipers (Cerastes), which are dangerously venomous (Topper, Ben-Oren & Kolodny, 2026). The puzzle is that all the scale-rasping vipers live north of the equator, while egg-eaters reach the southern tip of Africa. A 2026 computer-modelling study in PNAS suggests migrating predators that meet real vipers in the north could keep the bluff useful in the south. That idea is a hypothesis, not yet tested in the wild. Learn how to tell harmless lookalikes from dangerous snakes in our venomous or not ID guide and snake safety guide.

Where do egg-eating snakes live?

The common egg-eater ranges across much of sub-Saharan Africa, in savanna and grassland from sea level to over 1,500 m, but not in true desert or dense rainforest (Reptile Database). It is semi-arboreal. See more African snakes on our species page and habitats page, and how snakes swallow prey bigger than their heads on our anatomy page.

FAQ

How do egg-eating snakes eat eggs?

They swallow the egg whole, crack it in the throat on downward-pointing spikes from vertebrae 17 to 38, swallow the contents and spit out the crushed shell (Gartner & Greene, 2008).

Can an egg-eating snake swallow a chicken egg?

Large adults can. In lab trials, large egg-eaters ate eggs of every size offered, up to turkey eggs, and preferred the biggest, hardest ones (Krupa & Slabach, 2023).

Do egg-eating snakes have teeth?

Only a few small ones. The reduced teeth are part of their egg-eating anatomy and make them harmless to people (Gartner & Greene, 2008).

Are egg-eating snakes dangerous?

No. They have no venom, but they mimic the coiling, striking and scale-rasping display of saw-scaled vipers (Topper et al., 2026).

How often do egg-eating snakes eat?

Not often. Captive common egg-eaters needed food equal to only 61% of their body mass a year, and they ate mostly in spring and summer, when birds lay (Barends & Maritz, 2025).
