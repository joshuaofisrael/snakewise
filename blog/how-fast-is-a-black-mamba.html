How Fast Is a Black Mamba? mph, Myths and What Guinness Records | SnakeWise

A black mamba can reach about 10 to 12 mph (16 to 19 km/h) in short bursts over level ground. That is the Guinness World Records figure for the fastest land snake. Longer travel is slower, and horse-outrunning myths are exaggerations.

Quick facts

  • Species: black mamba, Dendroaspis polylepis, of sub-Saharan Africa.
  • Top speed (record sources): about 16 to 19 km/h (10 to 12 mph) in short bursts over level ground (Guinness World Records).
  • Typical cruising estimate: around 11 km/h (about 7 mph) over longer distances is often quoted in popular science summaries (Live Science, citing herpetologist commentary).
  • What speed is for: National Geographic notes black mambas use speed mainly to escape threats, not to chase prey.
  • Myth check: claims that black mambas outrun horses or reach speeds well above ~20 km/h are exaggerations (species accounts summarised on Wikipedia citing field sources).

How fast is a black mamba?

Guinness World Records lists the black mamba as the fastest land snake, with short bursts of 16 to 19 km/h (10 to 12 mph) over level ground. That is quick for a snake, but it is still slower than a fit person can sprint for a few seconds, and far slower than the horse-chasing stories that circulate online.

National Geographic puts the figure near 12.5 mph for top slithering speed. Live Science reports bursts up to about 12 mph with longer-distance averages near 7 mph. Wikipedia's species account, citing herpetological sources, states the snake cannot move more quickly than about 20 km/h (12 mph). Across these sources the consistent picture is short bursts in the low teens of miles per hour, not sustained highway speeds.

Why the internet gets this wrong

Search results sometimes crown the sidewinder as faster, or invent double-digit speeds that no peer-reviewed field measurement supports for a black mamba. Burst speed on open, level ground is what Guinness records. Sidewinding is a different gait optimised for sand, and comparing gait types as if they were the same race is misleading. For how sidewinders move, see our sidewinder locomotion post.

Black mambas can travel with much of the front body raised. That high crawl looks dramatic in photos and videos, which helps myths grow, but raised-body locomotion is about vigilance and striking reach, not proof of outrunning a vehicle.

Speed versus danger

Speed is only one reason the black mamba has a fierce reputation. It is a large, alert elapid with potent neurotoxic venom, and when cornered it may gap its black mouth, hiss and strike. National Geographic and field accounts emphasise that the snake usually prefers to flee, and that many scary stories exaggerate how eager it is to attack people. Speed helps it leave a threat zone quickly. It is not primarily a hunting sprint after people.

Key takeaways

  • Best supported top speed: about 10 to 12 mph (16 to 19 km/h) in short bursts.
  • Longer travel is slower; popular summaries often cite about 7 mph.
  • Speed is mainly for escape, not chasing people.
  • Horse-outrunning myths are not supported by the record figures.

FAQ

How fast is a black mamba in mph?

About 10 to 12 mph in short bursts on level ground, according to Guinness World Records and matching popular science summaries.

Is the black mamba the fastest snake?

Guinness lists it as the fastest land snake. Other snakes may look fast in different gaits, but the black mamba holds the commonly cited land-speed record.

Can a black mamba outrun a human?

Over a short flat burst it can match or beat a casual jog, but a sprinting adult human is usually faster. It cannot sustain car-like speeds.

Do black mambas use speed to hunt people?

No. Reliable accounts say they use speed to escape. Bites happen when a snake feels trapped or threatened, not from pursuit races.
