Can Snakes Hear? How Snakes Hear Without Ears

Yes, snakes can hear, but mostly low sounds and vibrations. They have no ear openings or eardrums, yet each has a working inner ear linked to the jaw, so vibration from the ground or from sound in the air reaches it through the skull. Pythons hear best at about 80 to 160 Hz, and rattlesnakes react to airborne tones.

Published 13 October 2026 · Last reviewed 13 October 2026

Quick facts

  • Do snakes have ears? Not outer ears or eardrums, but they do have an inner ear with a working cochlea.
  • How they pick up sound: mostly as vibration, carried from the jaw and skull through a small middle-ear bone (the columella) to the inner ear.
  • Best range: low pitches. Royal pythons were most sensitive at about 80 to 160 Hz.
  • Do they react to airborne sound? Yes. Rattlesnakes froze, jerked their heads and rattled when they heard tones with no ground vibration.

Can snakes hear?

Yes, snakes can hear, just not the way we do. They have no ear openings and no eardrum, which is why they were long called deaf. Inside the head, though, each snake has an inner ear with a functional cochlea. Researchers who played sounds to 19 Australian snakes put it plainly: while snakes lack external ears and an eardrum, "they are not deaf" (Zdenek et al., 2023).

What snakes hear best is low-frequency sound and vibration. Think of a footstep, a rumble, or the thud of something moving nearby, rather than birdsong or a conversation.

How do snakes hear without ears?

In most land animals, the eardrum and middle ear act as a bridge between thin air and the fluid of the inner ear. Snakes lost that bridge. Instead, the inner ear is linked through a tiny bone, the columella (the snake version of our stirrup bone), to the quadrate bone that hinges the lower jaw. When the jaw or skull vibrates, that movement is passed along to the fluid of the inner ear, and the snake "hears" it (Zdenek et al., 2023).

A snake lying on the ground rests its lower jaw on the soil, so ground vibrations travel straight into this system. Bruce Young's review of snake acoustics adds a second route: snakes also appear to detect vibration through the surface of the body, sometimes called somatic hearing (Young, 2003). What frequencies can snakes hear?

The clearest measurements come from royal pythons, also called ball pythons. Christensen and colleagues recorded brainstem responses in 11 of them and found the greatest sensitivity at low frequencies of about 80 to 160 Hz, both for vibration and for sound in the air (Christensen et al., 2012). For comparison, 100 Hz is a deep hum.

The same team then measured how much the snakes' heads shook when they were played sound at their hearing threshold. The air-borne sound made the head vibrate at least as much as the vibration stimulus did. Their conclusion was that pythons probably do not hear air pressure directly, the way an eardrum does. They feel the sound as it makes their head vibrate, and they are acutely sensitive to that vibration (Christensen et al., 2012).

Can snakes hear sound in the air, or only through the ground?

This is where the old "snakes only feel the ground" idea falls apart. In 2002, Young and Aguiar built a sound-isolation chamber and suspended western diamondback rattlesnakes in a steel mesh basket, so ground vibration was minimised. They then played tones concentrated between 200 and 400 Hz, just above the snakes' detection threshold. The snakes stopped moving, cut back their tongue-flicking, jerked their heads and rattled. At least one of these responses showed up in 92% of trials, the first experimental evidence that snakes respond to airborne sound (Young & Aguiar, 2002).

So the honest answer is both. Whether a snake is detecting the air pressure itself or the tiny vibrations that sound sets up in its skull, it does notice sound travelling through the air, as long as the sound is low and loud enough.

How do different snakes react to sound?

A 2023 study ran 304 trials on 19 snakes from five Australian genera in a soundproof room, using three bands of noise: 0 to 150 Hz (which also shook the floor), 150 to 300 Hz, and 300 to 450 Hz (Zdenek et al., 2023). The responses depended strongly on the type of snake:

  • Woma pythons (Aspidites) were the only group that became more likely to move when sound played, and they showed a trend to move towards the highest sound band.
  • Death adders, taipans and brown snakes were more likely to move away from the sound, which the authors read as possible avoidance.
  • Taipans became more likely to show defensive and cautious behaviour.

The authors suggest the common belief that snakes are deaf comes partly from their genuinely limited hearing range and partly from people missing the subtle ways snakes react (Zdenek et al., 2023). Can snakes tell where a sound is coming from?

Possibly, using their jaw. A snake's lower jaw has two sides that work in effect independently, and each rests on the ground. Friedel, Young and van Hemmen (2008) modelled the tiny surface waves that a prey animal's footsteps send through sand. Their physics model showed that jaw movement from those waves could give a snake realistic sensitivity and stereo-like precision, much like the timing difference between our two ears. That is a model, not a field measurement, but it fits how many snakes rest their heads flat on the ground while hunting.

Can snakes hear music?

A snake can probably detect the low, loud parts of music, such as bass, especially if it makes the floor or tank vibrate. None of the studies above tested whether snakes respond to music as music, so there is no evidence that snakes enjoy or follow a tune. For pet keepers, the practical point is that heavy bass and thumping near an enclosure reach a snake as vibration. FAQ

Are snakes deaf?

No. Snakes have no outer ear or eardrum, but they have a working inner ear and can sense low-frequency sound and vibration.

Where are a snake's ears?

There is no visible ear. The inner ear sits inside the skull, connected by a small bone called the columella to the quadrate bone at the hinge of the lower jaw.

What sounds can snakes hear best?

Low ones. Royal pythons were most sensitive at about 80 to 160 Hz, and rattlesnakes responded to tones between 200 and 400 Hz.

Can snakes feel footsteps?

Yes. Ground vibration passes through a snake's jaw and body to its inner ear, and researchers think this sharp vibration sense helps snakes detect both predators and prey (Christensen et al., 2012).
