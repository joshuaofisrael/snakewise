Snakes as Pets: The Basics

A pet snake can live 15 to 30+ years and needs a secure enclosure, a controlled heat gradient, correct humidity and whole-prey meals, usually frozen-thawed rodents. Calm, captive-bred species such as corn snakes, ball pythons, kingsnakes and Kenyan sand boas are common beginner choices.

Is a snake right for you?

Pet snakes can live 15–30+ years. Check local laws, find a reptile vet nearby, and be prepared to feed whole prey items.

Beginner-friendly species

Corn snakes, ball pythons, kingsnakes, and Kenyan sand boas are commonly recommended for their calm temperament and manageable size. Choose captive-bred animals.

Enclosure

Provide a secure, escape-proof enclosure sized for the adult snake, with hides at both warm and cool ends, a water bowl and appropriate substrate.

Heat and humidity

Snakes are ectotherms. Offer a thermal gradient using a thermostat-controlled heat source, and monitor with digital thermometers and a hygrometer. Needs differ by species.

Feeding

Most pet snakes eat appropriately sized frozen-thawed rodents. Never feed live prey unsupervised, as rodents can injure snakes.

Health

Signs of trouble include stuck shed, wheezing or mouth bubbles, mites, and prolonged refusal to eat. Wash hands after handling to prevent Salmonella.
