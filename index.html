Quick facts

  • Snakes have no eyelids; a clear scale called the spectacle (brille) protects each eye.
  • They smell with their forked tongue and the Jacobson's (vomeronasal) organ in the roof of the mouth.
  • Most snakes are harmless to people; only a minority of species have medically significant venom.
  • Snakes shed their whole skin, often in one piece, as they grow.

Snake FAQ

What should I do if a snake bites someone?

Call local emergency services or poison control; don't rely on this site. While waiting for help, keep the bitten person calm and as still as possible, take off rings, watches and tight clothing near the bite in case it swells, and remember the snake's colour and pattern (or photograph it from a safe distance). Do not cut or suck the bite, do not tie anything tightly around the limb, and do not try to catch or kill the snake. Treat every bite as an emergency, even if the snake looked harmless.

Sources: NHS: Snake bites; SnakeWise snake bite safety

How dangerous are snake bites worldwide?

Very. The World Health Organization estimates that about 5.4 million people are bitten by snakes each year, with 1.8 to 2.7 million envenomings and roughly 81,000 to 138,000 deaths. Venom can cause paralysis that stops breathing, bleeding disorders, kidney failure and severe tissue damage. Call local emergency services or poison control; don't rely on this site.

Sources: WHO: Snakebite envenoming fact sheet

Which snake has the most toxic venom?

In lab tests on mice, the inland taipan (Oxyuranus microlepidotus) of Australia has the most toxic venom of any snake, with an LD50 of about 0.01 mg/kg, and a 1983 study measured an average of 17.3 mg of venom delivered per strike. It is shy and lives in remote areas, so human bites are rare. If anyone is bitten by any snake, call local emergency services or poison control; don't rely on this site.

Sources: Morrison et al. 1983, Journal of Toxicology: Clinical Toxicology; SnakeWise: How venomous is the inland taipan?

Can snakes hear?

Yes, but not the way we do. Snakes have no outer ear or eardrum, yet they have an inner ear connected to the jaw. Tests on royal pythons found they were most sensitive to low frequencies of about 80 to 160 Hz and mostly sense sound as vibration, so they are far better at feeling ground vibrations than hearing airborne sound.

Sources: Christensen et al. 2012, Journal of Experimental Biology; SnakeWise: Can snakes hear?

How long can a snake go without eating?

Much longer than most animals. In one study, ball pythons, ratsnakes and western diamondback rattlesnakes survived 168 days without food by cutting their resting metabolism by up to 72% and burning fat while sparing protein, and some snakes are reported to survive up to about 2 years. A pet snake that refuses food for a long time should still be checked by a reptile vet.

Sources: McCue 2007, Zoology; SnakeWise: How long can a snake go without eating?

What is the smallest snake in the world?

The Barbados threadsnake (Leptotyphlops carlae, now Tetracheilostoma carlae), described in 2008, is the smallest known snake, with adults about 100 mm (4 inches) long. Limited evidence suggests females lay a single, very elongated egg.

Sources: Hedges 2008, Zootaxa; SnakeWise: What is the smallest snake in the world?
