How do snakes move?

Snakes move without legs in four main ways: lateral undulation (S-shaped waves pushing off the ground), concertina (bunching and extending, used in tunnels and for climbing), rectilinear (a slow, straight crawl using the belly scales) and sidewinding (used on loose sand). A few tree snakes can even glide.

Lateral undulation: the S-shaped slither

The most familiar method: the body forms S-shaped waves and pushes against rocks, plants and bumps in the ground. Also used for swimming.

Concertina: how snakes climb and move through tunnels

Used in tunnels or for climbing. The snake anchors part of its body, extends the front forward, then pulls the rear up, like an accordion.

Rectilinear: how snakes crawl in a straight line

A slow, straight-line 'caterpillar' crawl. Belly scales are lifted and pulled forward in waves. Common in heavy-bodied snakes like large vipers and pythons.

Sidewinding: how snakes move on loose sand

On loose sand, some vipers lift sections of their body and move diagonally, leaving J-shaped tracks while touching only a little ground at once.

Snake movement FAQ

What is the most common way snakes move?

Lateral undulation (serpentine movement) is the most familiar method: the body forms S-shaped waves and pushes against rocks, plants and bumps in the ground. It is also used for swimming.

How do snakes climb?

Snakes use concertina movement in tunnels or for climbing. The snake anchors part of its body, extends the front forward, then pulls the rear up, like an accordion.

How do snakes move in a straight line?

Can snakes fly?
