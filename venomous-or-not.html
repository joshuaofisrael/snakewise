Venomous or not? Snake identification guide by region

Pick your region to see the main venomous snakes and the harmless snakes most often mistaken for them. No single feature (head shape, pupil shape, colour) reliably identifies a venomous snake worldwide. If you are unsure, assume it is venomous, keep your distance and never handle it. North America (USA & Canada)

SnakeStatusKey features
RattlesnakesVenomousRattle or button on tail tip, heat pit between eye and nostril, vertical pupils, heavy body.
CopperheadVenomousTan to copper body with dark hourglass-shaped crossbands (narrow on the back, wide on the sides).
Cottonmouth (water moccasin)VenomousThick dark body, white mouth lining shown when threatened, often swims with whole body riding on the surface.
Coral snakesVenomousRed, yellow and black bands where red touches yellow (US species only; the rhyme fails elsewhere).
Gopher / bull snakeHarmlessShakes tail and hisses to imitate a rattlesnake but has no rattle; round pupils.
Northern water snakeHarmlessOften mistaken for cottonmouth or copperhead; round pupils, swims with mainly the head above water.
Milk snakes & scarlet kingsnakeHarmlessRed bands touch black bands, not yellow.

United Kingdom & Europe

SnakeStatusKey features
Adder (common European viper)VenomousDark zigzag stripe along the back, vertical pupils, red or copper eyes. The UK's only venomous snake.
Asp viper & nose-horned viperVenomousSouthern and central Europe; upturned snout or 'horn', vertical pupils.
Grass snakeHarmlessYellow-and-black collar behind head, round pupils, often near water; may play dead.
Smooth snakeHarmlessRare in the UK; slender, grey-brown with small paired spots and a dark stripe through the eye; round pupils.

Australia

SnakeStatusKey features
Eastern brown snakeVenomousHighly variable colour (tan to almost black), slender and fast. Colour alone cannot identify it.
Tiger snakeVenomousOften banded but can be plain; flattens neck when threatened.
Red-bellied black snakeVenomousGlossy black back, red or pink lower sides; usually near water.
Death addersVenomousShort, stout, triangular head, thin tail tip used as a lure.
Carpet pythonHarmlessNon-venomous constrictor; heat pits along lips, intricate blotched pattern; can still bite.
Common tree snakeHarmlessSlender, green-olive with yellow belly, very large eyes.

South Asia (India, Sri Lanka, Pakistan, Bangladesh)

SnakeStatusKey features
Spectacled cobraVenomousRaises and spreads a hood, often with a spectacle-like mark on the back of the hood.
Common kraitVenomousGlossy black or blue-black with thin white paired bands; active at night, often enters homes.
Russell's viperVenomousThree rows of dark brown chain-like spots on a tan body; loud hiss.
Saw-scaled viperVenomousSmall, rubs coils to make a rasping sound; arrow or cross mark on head.
Indian rat snakeHarmlessLarge, fast, often mistaken for a cobra but has no hood.
Common wolf snakeHarmlessBrown with pale bands; resembles a krait but is not venomous.

Sub-Saharan Africa

SnakeStatusKey features
Puff adderVenomousThick body with pale chevrons, relies on camouflage and may not move away.
Black mambaVenomousLong, grey-brown (not black), coffin-shaped head, inky-black mouth lining.
Green mambasVenomousBright green, slender tree snakes in East, West and southern Africa.
BoomslangVenomousRear-fanged tree snake with huge eyes; colour varies by sex. Bites are rare but serious.
Spitting cobrasVenomousCan spray venom toward the eyes from a distance; protect eyes and back away.
Spotted bush snakeHarmlessGreen with black spots on front half; often confused with green mambas.
Brown house snakeHarmlessBrown with pale lines on the head; common around homes, eats rodents.

Central & South America

SnakeStatusKey features
Lancehead (fer-de-lance, Bothrops)VenomousTriangular head, pit between eye and nostril, X- or triangle-shaped markings; a leading cause of bites.
BushmastersVenomousVery large pit vipers with rough, keeled scales; live in rainforests.
Coral snakesVenomousMany banding patterns; colour rhymes do NOT work here. Treat any banded red snake with caution.

Snake identification FAQ

Are there venomous snakes in the UK?

Yes, one: the adder is the UK's only native venomous snake. It has a dark zigzag stripe along its back and vertical pupils. The UK's other two native snakes, the grass snake and the smooth snake, are harmless. Anyone bitten by an adder should get medical help straight away.

How can I tell a coral snake from a milk snake?

In the United States, coral snakes have red bands touching yellow bands, while harmless milk snakes and scarlet kingsnakes have red bands touching black. This rule only works for US species; in Central and South America coral snake patterns vary, so treat any red-banded snake with caution.

Do all venomous snakes have triangular heads and slit pupils?

No. Cobras, kraits, mambas and coral snakes are dangerously venomous yet have round pupils and fairly slim heads, and many harmless snakes flatten their heads to look bigger when threatened. No single feature identifies a venomous snake worldwide.

Is a snake that shakes its tail always a rattlesnake?

No. Gopher snakes, bull snakes and some other harmless snakes vibrate their tails in dry leaves to imitate a rattlesnake. Only rattlesnakes have a true rattle (or a button) on the tail tip, but keep your distance either way.

Which harmless snakes are mistaken for cobras and kraits in South Asia?

The Indian rat snake is often mistaken for a cobra but never spreads a hood. The common wolf snake looks like a krait but is not venomous.

What should I do if I can't identify a snake?

Assume it is venomous, keep your distance, never handle it or try to kill it, and keep children and pets away. Local wildlife services or herpetological societies can often identify a snake from a photo taken from a safe distance.
