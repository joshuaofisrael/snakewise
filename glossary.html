Snake glossary

By the SnakeWise editorial team · Last reviewed: 20 November 2026 · Editorial policy

Plain-English definitions of common snake and herpetology terms, from brumation and ecdysis to elapid, scute and viviparous.

Jump to: Brumation · Colubrid · Constriction · Ecdysis · Ectotherm · Elapid · Fang · Heat pit · Herpetology · Jacobson's organ · Neurotoxin · Oviparous · Viviparous · Ophiophagous · Scute · Spectacle · Venom · Viper
Brumation
A cold-season slowdown in reptiles, similar to hibernation. How snakes warm up and cool down is covered in are snakes cold-blooded?, and cold places are on the habitats guide.
Colubrid
A member of Colubridae, the largest snake family. That label is not a safety test: some rear-fanged species in the family can cause serious bites. Use the ID guide, and if you are unsure, keep away.
Constriction
Subduing prey by coiling and squeezing. A 2015 study of boas and rats, and what it did and did not show, is on the venom vs constriction page.
Ecdysis
Shedding the outer skin. The outer layer is dead tissue, so it is replaced rather than repaired. The studies and the cloudy-eye phase are in why snakes shed.
Ectotherm
An animal that warms and cools with its surroundings instead of holding a steady warm body temperature the way a bird or mammal does. The plain-language version is are snakes cold-blooded?
Elapid
A front-fanged venomous snake such as a cobra, mamba, sea snake or coral snake. Fang types are set out on the venom page. Venom labels may be wrong.
Fang
A tooth used to deliver venom. The measured record, and why viper fangs fold, is which snake has the longest fangs?

Heat pit
An organ that senses infrared radiation from warm prey. Pit vipers, pythons and boas have them. The papers are in can snakes see heat?
Herpetology
The study of reptiles and amphibians. A short list of papers written up for students is on the research page.
Jacobson's organ
The vomeronasal organ in the roof of the mouth. The forked tongue delivers chemicals to it. How that sampling works is in why snakes flick their tongues.
Neurotoxin
A toxin that acts on the nervous system. One snake whose venom is discussed in those terms, with the studies named, is the inland taipan.
Oviparous
Laying eggs. King cobra nests are one studied case of a snake that lays a clutch and then stays with it.
Viviparous
Giving birth to live young rather than laying a shelled egg. Snakes do both, depending on the species. Cold-climate notes are on the habitats guide.
Ophiophagous
Eating other snakes. The king cobra profile is the example on this site: it eats mainly other snakes.
Scute
A large scale, especially the broad belly scales that meet the ground. How those scales fit the skeleton is on the anatomy page.
Spectacle (brille)
The clear covering over a snake's eye. Snakes have no movable eyelids, so they cannot blink. The covering is shed with the skin. See do snakes have eyelids?
Venom
A toxic secretion that is injected, usually by a bite. That is different from a poison that harms you if you eat or touch it. Venom labels may be wrong. The distinction, and one studied exception, is on venom vs constriction. If you are bitten, use the safety page and call emergency services.
Viper
A member of Viperidae. The long fangs hinge and fold against the roof of the mouth. How that compares with fixed front fangs is in the longest-fangs article.

These are short definitions on purpose. Where a line points to an article, that article carries the studies and the date they were checked. Reviewed 20 November 2026. The colubrid warning is a safety rule of this site, not a count of species.
